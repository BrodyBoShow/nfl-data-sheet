# P10 — Scheme

**Status: CLOSED 2026-10-20 as not buildable (user). A close, not a deferral.** Nothing
was built. There are two independent reasons, and **either alone closes P10** (user,
2026-10-20):
- **Measurement** (sections 1 and 2): the inputs don't exist in season, and the signal
  isn't there even with them. Neither goes away with more weeks of data or next
  season's release.
- **Licensing** (section 3): the only free in-season personnel data is a provider's own
  charting, published as an HTML tool. Taking it would break the provenance rule.

Created 2026-10-07 by P7's scope reduction (user): Scheme moved out of P7 unbuilt.

## Why it closed

The question it was meant to answer (user, 2026-10-19): matchup-specific ratings, e.g.
"how do the Commanders play against the personnel Kittle is efficient in", rather than
player and team ratings as separate facts.

### 1. The inputs don't exist in season. That's structural, not scheduling.
- Personnel, coverage type, man/zone and routes come only from nflverse's participation
  file, released after the postseason.
  - Checked live 2026-10-20: the `pbp_participation` release's latest file is
    `_2025` (updated 2026-02-10). There is no 2026 file, and there won't be one until
    about February.
  - nflreadpy refuses the current season outright (`docs/sources.md`, "P7 research").
- FTN's public charting has no coverage, personnel or alignment fields.
- No source we have gives slot vs wide alignment, in season or historically.
  `pass_location` is where the ball went, not where the receiver lined up.
- The staged `ftn` table has no possession team. Its per-play flags reach a team only
  through `player_game_pbp`'s per-player sums.

### 2. The signal isn't there, even with the inputs
Measured 2026-10-20, read-only, on 2022–2025 REG (128 defense-seasons, 133,636 plays).
Play scope and roles came from the collector's own `_player_play_scope` and
`_aggregate_player_game_pbp` definitions. Receiver position came from `players.position`.

**The defense side.** A split's own signal is (split EPA/play − the defense's overall
EPA/play for that play type), i.e. what the split says beyond the overall rating.
- First 6 games against games 7–17, pooled r:
  - In-season splits (TE, RB and WR targets, middle and outside, play action, blitzed,
    inside and edge runs, stacked box): **−0.13 to +0.12.**
  - Participation-only splits (personnel 11/12/21/13, man, zone, TE vs zone, TE vs 12):
    **−0.22 to +0.17.**
  - Every 95% bootstrap interval includes zero.
- Split-half at 13 games: **at most +0.27.** The in-season splits are all at most +0.13.
  PA targets read +0.01, PA dropbacks −0.10, blitzed dropbacks −0.19.
- Year over year (full season, same defense, next season):
  - Raw values: **−0.13 to +0.16** across every split and baseline.
  - The split beyond the overall: −0.12 to +0.11.
  - **Overall defensive EPA per dropback: r = 0.05**, unadjusted for opponent.
- Plays needed before a split's difference is half signal: 446 middle targets, about
  1,200 inside runs, about 5,900 WR targets. For TE targets, play action, blitzed
  dropbacks and edge runs, no difference was detectable at all. Entering week 7, 2026
  stored data gives the thinnest defense 31 TE targets, 22 RB targets, 17 middle
  targets, and 7 runs at one gap.

**The player side, year over year** (season s → s+1, same player):

| Split | r | Pairs |
|---|---|---|
| EPA/target vs man minus vs zone, WR and TE (≥20 targets in each) | +0.02 | 208 |
| Same, TE only (≥15 in each) | −0.09 | 67 |
| EPA/target middle minus outside, WR and TE | +0.01 | 86 |
| EPA/target play action minus not, WR and TE | 0.00 | 132 |
| TE EPA/target in 12 minus in 11 personnel | +0.28 | 19 (CI not computed) |

So "the personnel Kittle is efficient in" isn't a persistent trait in any split tested.

*(Correction, 2026-10-20: the 2026-10-20 investigation report gave the defense range
first 6 → rest as "−0.13 to +0.19". The measured extremes are −0.22 (man) and +0.17
(21 personnel), and −0.13 to +0.12 for the in-season splits. The user's close message
attributed +0.01 and 0.00 to a defense split-half. Those two are the player-side
year-over-year figures above.)*

### 3. Licensing: the in-season alternatives are providers' work product
From the user's licensing research (2026-10-20). Recorded as the user gave it. The
source URLs and any terms text weren't part of the research summary, so no clause is
quoted here.
- **Root cause: the league stopped supplying it.** NFL Next Gen Stats stopped providing
  participation data during the 2023 season. Losing in-season participation wasn't an
  nflverse choice.
  - That matches the attribution split in P7's source inventory: participation is
    "NFL NextGenStats via nflverse" through 2022 and "FTN Data via nflverse" from 2023.
- **nflverse's replacement is FTN, licensed for post-season release only.** 2026's
  file arrives about February 2027. That matches section 1's live check (latest file
  `_2025`, updated 2026-02-10).
- **Free in-season personnel data exists on the web, but not in a form we could take.**
  Sharp Football Analysis and SumerSports publish it, but:
  - only as HTML tools, with no API or CSV;
  - and both are the providers' own analysis of their own charting, not a league
    statistic.
- **Why that's a no, separately from the measurement:** taking it means scraping a
  provider's site and republishing their work product. The project's provenance rule
  forbids exactly that. We take PFR and NGS data only as nflverse release assets, never
  from the providers' sites (CLAUDE.md layer rules; `docs/sources.md`, nflverse bulk →
  License).
  - The user's assessment: it's a weaker position than ESPN's (`docs/phases/P8.md`,
    "Filed: ESPN's terms vs. live ESPN collectors", D1/D2). Claude's reading of why, not
    the user's words: ESPN's injuries feed carries the league's injury designations,
    while a personnel chart is the provider's own product.
- **NFL Pro doesn't fill the gap.** It has box counts, not personnel packages, and it's
  behind NFL+ Premium, which the $0/month constraint rules out anyway.

### What IS stable: tendency, not efficiency
First 6 games → games 7–17, pooled, with 95% CI:

| Tendency | r | Next season | In season? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blitz rate (FTN, of dropbacks) | **0.66** [0.52, 0.76] | — | yes |
| Man rate (of labeled dropbacks) | 0.72 [0.64, 0.78] | — | **no**, participation only |
| Cover-3 rate | 0.65 [0.55, 0.74] | — | **no**, participation only |
| Share of runs faced inside (lg/mid/rg) | **0.48** [0.33, 0.61] | 0.48 | yes |
| PA share of dropbacks faced | 0.43 [0.26, 0.58] | 0.22 | yes |
| Share of targets faced to the middle | **0.41** [0.26, 0.53] | 0.48 | yes |
| 12-personnel share faced | 0.38 [0.17, 0.54] | 0.32 | **no** |
| Stacked-box rate (FTN, of runs) | 0.28 [0.10, 0.46] | — | yes |
| Share of targets to RBs | 0.22 [0.04, 0.39] | 0.32 | yes |
| Share of targets to TEs | 0.15 [−0.02, 0.31] | 0.29 | yes |

What a defense does, and where offenses go against it, persists. How well a defense
does against one kind of play, beyond its overall rating, doesn't.
- The in-season half of this (blitz rate, target location, run direction) is carried
  into the readability work as tendency context. It's labeled tendency, never efficiency.
  It isn't P10 scope reopened.

### Method caveats
- Unadjusted for opponent.
- 2022–2025 only: four seasons, FTN era.
- Man/zone labels drift by season. The man share of labeled dropbacks was 29% / 41% /
  48% / 31% (2022–2025). That's P7 open item 11's question, now measured on dropbacks.
  2022 labels 89% of dropbacks, 2023+ about 99.6%.
- A pooled r over 128 defense-seasons has a standard error of about 0.09.
- Split-half was Spearman-Brown on odd vs even games. It's meaningless for negative r,
  which several splits gave at 6 games.
- The scripts were scratch and weren't kept in the repo. The method above is what they
  did.

## What it would have built (for the record)
- A Scheme analyst (`scheme.py`), team-level, writing `signals`:
  - pass rate over expected, neutral pace;
  - play-action/motion/RPO/screen rates, box counts, blitz/pressure rate;
  - approximate personnel;
  - run defense by gap, coverage-proxy splits;
  - participation-derived man/zone and shell tendencies (`_hist`).
- It had no registry entries, no architecture-ready inputs (no `xpass` or game state at
  team-game grain, no team on FTN rows, no team-season participation aggregate), and an
  unmeasured 16.5 MB/season storage estimate.
- None of it is carried forward except the in-season tendency subset noted above.
