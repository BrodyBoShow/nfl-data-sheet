## Start blocker: the narrator vs. Sports Reference's AI clause (filed 2026-09-25)

**P8 can't start the narrator until this is decided.** Don't design around it before
then.
- Sports Reference's Terms of Use §5 (quoted verbatim in `docs/sources.md`, nflverse
  bulk → License) bars using its content "for purposes of training, fine-tuning,
  prompting, or instructing artificial intelligence models or technologies in any
  manner".
- The narrator prompts an LLM with a card's content. From P7 on, cards and player views
  can carry PFR-derived values (`snaps`, `pfr_advstats`), including Efficiency's use of
  snaps for O-line continuity.
- Decide one of these before any narrator code:
  - **(a)** PFR-derived values are excluded from everything the narrator is prompted
    with. That needs a provenance tag per value, precise enough to enforce.
  - **(b)** The narrator is dropped.
- This concerns only the narrator. LLM news parsing reads news items, not PFR data, and
  isn't affected.

## Filed: the dispatcher runs at ~6% of its designed tick rate (2026-10-01)
- **Designed:** `.github/workflows/dispatcher.yml` cron `7,22,37,52 * * * *`, which is
  96 ticks a day.
- **Observed:** 40 ticks over 2026-09-24..09-30, **5.7 a day, 5.9% of designed**. That's
  counted from the synthesizer, which runs on every tick.
  - The average gap is about 4 h. The longest per day ran 5 h 01 m to 8 h 23 m
    (09-28).
  - The workflow file already records the cause qualitatively: GitHub's free-tier
    scheduled triggers are best-effort ("over ~3 hours at a 10-min cron, only 1
    scheduled run actually fired").
- **What this rate shapes:**
  - **Grading lag.** Grades land on the first tick after a final. P5's settling rule
    adds about one tick (`docs/phases/P5.md`, "ticks observed 2–7h apart").
  - **Lock lead time.** A lock happens on the first tick after `locks_from`, so its
    lead before kickoff varies with when ticks arrive. A game can kick off unlocked,
    which the web shows as "NOT LOCKED · kicked off without a lock", and the auditor's
    `check_projection_locks` alerts on it.
  - **Egress projections.** Every per-month egress figure in `docs/phases/P7.md` step 9
    (b) assumes the current ~5.7 ticks a day.
    - Analyst runs gated on "a collector wrote rows" scale with tick count. At the
      designed rate they'd scale up to ~17×, and so would that egress.
    - Only a freshness gate keyed on the analysts' own inputs decouples runs from
      ticks: mandatory, decided 2026-10-01.
- **Not assessed:** whether to change how ticks are delivered, e.g. an external
  scheduler calling `workflow_dispatch`. That belongs with this phase's dispatcher
  tuning, and it would raise any tick-scaled cost.
  - *(2026-10-05: tick-scaled cost is much lower than assumed here. After the server-side
    diff, recurring egress is 6.8 MB on 04 Oct (hover value) at 5 ticks, roughly 1.4
    MB/tick. That's a day's total divided by a tick count. At the designed 96 ticks/day
    it's still ~3.9 GB/month before gated analyst runs, near the 5 GB tier. See
    `P7.md`, step 9, "Egress result consequences", item 4.)*
- **INVERTED (user, 2026-10-05): the low tick rate is what keeps the budget solvent.**
  - This section filed GitHub's cron unreliability (5.7 ticks a day against a designed
    96) as a defect. At ~1.4 MB/tick, raising the rate to the design would cost ~3.9
    GB/month before any analyst run. **A planned fix is now a planned cost.**
  - **The caveat that decides it: 1.4 MB/tick is an AVERAGE, not a MARGINAL cost.** It's
    a day's total over a tick count.
    - Much of what ran that day doesn't scale with ticks. The web's page regenerations
      don't, for one.
    - The 6.8 MB was read on the All Projects filter, so it also includes the other
      projects' ~1.3 MB/day (`P6.md`, Open 1, "OUTCOME").
    - The number that would decide whether the tick rate can rise is the **marginal
      cost of one additional tick**. That's almost certainly far below 1.4 MB.
  - **The measurement to take IF tick rate becomes a product decision.** It might: tick
    rate sets lock lead time and grading lag. **Not now.** Method, either:
    - compare days with different tick counts, on the meter's daily bars; or
    - isolate the per-tick jobs' own reads from the day's total (pg_stat, or a logging
      cursor as in `P7.md` step 9 (b)).
  - The input gate makes extra ticks cheaper too. A gated analyst on a tick with no
    input change costs ~300 bytes (one digest read), not a full read.

## Scope
- Live game collector: ESPN scoreboard/game summary (unofficial). Stores live_games,
  live_box, espn_lines. Polls as one looping job per game window, not a new job every
  few minutes.
- Dispatcher tuning: refine the idle-window logic built in P1 against real usage data
  from prior phases; confirm GitHub Actions minutes stay within free tier.
- **Intel collector** (`intel.py`). **Moved here from P7, 2026-10-07 (user):** a scope
  reduction in P7, which never started it (`P7.md`, "Scope reduction"). It's a collector,
  and this phase is collectors.
  - ESPN NFL news feed, official team RSS where available, Sleeper trending players as a
    news-spike indicator. Dedupe by URL + text hash.
  - **Rule-based tagging only** (keywords + crosswalk name matching): team, player_id,
    category (injury, depth chart, suspension, coaching, signing/release/trade). **No
    LLM calls in the tagging path.**
  - It arrives in the one phase that allows an LLM. In P7 the no-LLM rule (CLAUDE.md,
    Phases 1–7) covered this tagging. Here only its own done-when line below does, so
    the optional LLM parsing stays a separate upgrade on top of it.
  - Storage (`P7.md`, "Storage design", volume unverified): 5–20 MB/season, ~1–2 MB with
    30-day retention.
- **Optional, small-cost exceptions only:**
  - LLM news parsing (Haiku batch, changed items only) as an upgrade to the Intel
    collector's rule-based tagging.
  - Matchup narrator: on-demand prose generated from one card's signals, cached per game
    per day, cannot introduce numbers not already on the card. Requires
    `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`.
