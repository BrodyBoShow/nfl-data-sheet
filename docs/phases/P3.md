## Correctness item: ESPN absence-clears long-term designations (open, 2026-09-25)

**Mechanism.** ESPN's injuries endpoint returns only the **25 most recently updated
entries per team** (verified live 2026-09-25; `docs/sources.md`, Availability).
- IR/Out/Doubtful are exactly the designations that stop updating, so those players age
  out of the window.
- `detect_cleared` then clears them after two consecutive absences, as if they had
  recovered. **Absence from a recency window is evidence of staleness, not recovery.**
- This is the mechanism behind the symptom noted during P3's build, that ESPN "drops
  IR/PUP/Reserve players once they're old news"
  (`availability_impact._resolve_current_state` docstring). It's not an editorial
  choice by ESPN; it's a size cap.
- The outage guard can't see it, because the feed is always full-size (800).

**Measured, 2026-09-18..25:**
- Of ESPN's 281 absence-clears: 234 were `Active` (harmless), 39 `Injured Reserve`,
  5 `Questionable`, 3 `Out`, 0 `Doubtful`.
- For the 42 IR/Out clears, Sleeper still lists 35 as injured today, 1 is Sleeper-cleared
  now, 6 have no Sleeper row, and **none has logged a snap since**. 3 later reappeared on
  ESPN: 2 still IR, 1 Questionable.
- Masked in the analyst: an ESPN clear never overrides an active Sleeper designation.
  The unmasked exposure is the 6 IR players with no Sleeper row (plus 2 Questionable).
- Genuine exits from IR/Out/Doubtful **are posted as fresh ESPN entries**: 191 in the
  same 8 days (132 to Active, 59 to Questionable). A recovery is itself an update, so
  it lands at the top of the window.

**Proposed rule (not implemented; awaiting the user):**
1. **Collector** (`pipeline/core/injury_changelog.py`, shared by the collector, backfill,
   and verify scripts):
   - For source `espn`, a player whose last written designation is `Injured Reserve`,
     `Out`, or `Doubtful` is never cleared by absence.
   - `detect_cleared` still counts their misses in `injury_presence`, with `last_seen_at`
     preserved, so the staleness is recorded. But no `is_cleared` row is written.
   - They leave that state only through a listed ESPN row, the path that caught 191
     exits above.
   - Sleeper keeps the 2-miss rule: it's a full dump, not a window, and its clears
     checked out as real (157 of 162 on 2026-09-25 had no injury status in a live
     fetch). `Questionable` also keeps the absence rule, since it's a weekly designation
     that expires after the game.
2. **Analyst** (`availability_impact._resolve_current_state`):
   - An ESPN IR/Out/Doubtful state whose player is absent from ESPN's latest polls
     (`injury_presence.consecutive_misses >= 2`) is **stale**.
   - A stale ESPN state yields to Sleeper whenever Sleeper has a current row for the
     player, flagged or cleared.
   - It yields to `snaps`: at least one snap in a game after ESPN's `last_seen_at`
     clears it.
   - Otherwise it stands, and its age (days since ESPN last listed the player) is
     carried alongside.
   - Both inputs are stored tables the analyst already reads or may read (L2 rule).
3. **History:** the 42 existing sticky clears stay as audit rows unless the user wants a
   one-off correction. Under the rule, `replay_history` would no longer produce them.

**What it prevents:** 42 of the 281 clears (15%): 39 IR, 3 Out. That includes the 6 IR
players with no Sleeper row, the unmasked exposure.

**What it costs when a player genuinely recovers.** A recovery is missed only if it
never appears as a listed ESPN entry while we're polling:
- (a) posted and aged out between two polls (needs 25+ newer updates on that team in
  ~4–5 h; not observed);
- (b) a collector outage longer than the window's turnover;
- (c) the player is released, traded, or retires while listed, and ESPN drops him
  without an `Active`.

In each case the player reads as injured when healthy until Sleeper or a snap says
otherwise. The error direction flips: from false-healthy (today) to false-injured, and
it's bounded by the first game he plays. Storage cost is negligible (~5 fewer cleared
rows a day).

## Scope
- Availability collector: ESPN injuries, Sleeper players (≤1/day). NFL.com injury report
  scraping stayed out of scope for this session. Stores `injuries` (append-only
  snapshot log, both sources) and `transactions` (team/designation changes, derived by
  diffing consecutive snapshots). No `practice_status` table — live verification found
  neither source exposes structured per-day participation data; see
  `docs/architecture.md` §Deviations and `docs/sources.md`'s Availability section.
- Availability impact analyst: snap-share redistribution, replacement depth-rank delta
  (depth-chart order only, not a quality estimate — see `docs/signals.md`), OL/secondary
  cluster counts, practice-trend risk (a designation-change ordinal, not real practice
  participation). Consumes raw snap shares (from the `snaps` table, populated by the
  Phase 2 nflverse bulk collector) as the redistribution baseline, since Usage/role's
  more refined target/carry shares aren't built until P7. Revisit whether to switch to
  Usage's shares once P7 exists.
- Source verification: ESPN + Sleeper endpoints live-called, fixtures saved,
  `docs/sources.md` updated from UNVERIFIED to VERIFIED with real shapes/limits.
- `player_id_crosswalk.sleeper_id` coverage gap found and partially fixed: `id_spine.py`
  now self-heals it (fill-null-only, from Sleeper's own self-reported `gsis_id`); the
  Availability collector stays read-only against the crosswalk.
- Two shared modules pulled out during this phase, not duplicated per-collector:
  `pipeline/core/team_aliases.py` (retired-franchise codes + ESPN's `WSH`/`WAS` quirk,
  also now used by `nflverse_bulk.py`) and `pipeline/core/schedule.py`
  (`resolve_season_week` — derives season/week from the `games` table for sources with no
  week of their own, rather than trusting a run's `ctx.week`).
